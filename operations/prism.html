Chipload 0.37.0 user guide

Prism

Prism leaves closed shapes standing as roofs: their sides slope up from the edges to a sharp ridge along the centre line, as hand-chiselled raised letters. It is the opposite of a V-carve, which sinks the shape into the board, and it uses the same drawing and the same V-bit.

Select the shapes, with the shapes inside them that are holes (an O’s counter), then press S over the canvas and R, or choose Operation > New Prism, or New operation in a part’s right-click menu in the Browser.

How it cuts

The V-bit’s tip runs once round each outline, going as deep as the shape is wide there: the radius of the largest circle inside the shape that touches the outline at that point, over the tangent of half the bit’s angle. The bit’s inner flank then reaches the surface exactly on the centre line, so one lap round the outline cuts the whole roof.

  • A stroke 10 mm wide comes out as a ridge 5 mm high with a 90° bit, 8.7 mm with a 60° bit.
  • At a sharp outside corner the edge comes up to the surface, so corners end in points, as a chiselled serif does. Where strokes meet, the junction rises to a peak.
  • With a flat-tipped bit, the tip runs half its width outside the line, so the edge has no step.
  • Each lap starts at its shallowest point, at the surface where the shape has a corner.

Wider than the bit: where a shape is wider than the bit’s cone can bring to a ridge, its edges go as deep as the cone allows and the middle stays flat, at the surface. A note says which shape. Max depth does the same on purpose: bevelled raised shapes with a flat top, every edge no deeper than you set.

Round the shapes: the bit’s other flank cuts outwards, leaving a V-shaped trench round each shape as deep as its edge. Leave it for a carved-in look, or clear the background first with a pocket as deep as the deepest edge (the report says how deep) for shapes standing on a flat background. Where shapes stand so close that one’s trench reaches the next, that shape’s lower edge is cut into: a note names the two shapes and how much deeper the edge goes there; space them further apart, or set a max depth.

Open shapes are skipped with a note.

Settings

Setting
Toola V-bit with its angle; any other tool is refused
Start depthwhere the surface is: 0 for the stock top, more for a prism in the floor of a pocket
Max depthoff: as deep as the shapes and the bit’s cone need. On: no deeper than this, for bevelled shapes with a flat top. Beneath it, how deep the edges can go with the bit
Step downoff: one lap. On: equal passes, each the same lap no deeper than its level
Directionclimb or conventional
Feed, Plunge feedmm/min

A new prism takes the project’s first V-bit, no max depth, the tool’s step down, climb.

The report gives the deepest edge (“the deepest edge is 6 mm”). The setup sheet says “Prism, climb”, with the depths “0 to at most 6.35” (the bit’s cone or the max depth).

Command line

chipload op add part.chipload prism --shapes 1,2,3
chipload op add part.chipload prism --layer LETTERS --max-depth 3
chipload set part.chipload operations.0.max_depth=2.5

--max-depth gives a flat top; a prism takes no --depth or --through, as its depth follows from the shapes.

In the file

"type": "prism" in the project file. Lengths are in mm, feeds in mm/min. A project with a prism opens in Chipload 0.37.0 and later.
