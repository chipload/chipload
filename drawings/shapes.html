Chipload 0.40.0 user guide

Drawing shapes

Chipload draws the parts it cuts, with or without a DXF:

  • Circle (key C): the centre, then a point on it. Two more ways, from the small arrow on its button (below):
    • Two points: the two ends of a diameter (type a length after the first for the diameter).
    • Three points: three points on it, such as three corners it must pass through. Three points on one line make no circle, and the status bar says so.
  • Rectangle (R): two opposite corners. It keeps its centre, length and width, a rotation, and a corner radius (0: sharp corners). Two more ways to draw it, from the small arrow on its button (below):
    • Centre and corner: the centre, then a corner.
    • Aligned (three points): the two ends of one edge, at any angle, then its width (a click on either side of the edge, or type it). The rectangle is turned to the edge.
  • Polyline (L): points joined by straight or arc segments, open or closed. Click the first point again to close it; Enter or a double-click finishes it open.
    • Arcs while drawing: press A (or Arc on the status bar) and the next sides are arcs, each going on smoothly from the side before it; the click sets where the arc ends. L (or Line) goes back to lines. Shift holds the arc’s sweep to 15° steps (a quarter or half circle), and the readout shows its radius and sweep. The first side needs a direction to go on from, so it is a line, unless you start on the end of an open drawn shape: then the arc goes on from that shape. A point straight behind can’t be reached by an arc. Each new polyline starts with lines. On the command line, t in --bulges is such an arc: chipload shape add P polyline --open --points "0,0 40,0 60,20 60,40" --bulges "0 t t".
    • Rounded corners: each corner can have a Radius, an arc tangent to both sides, beside an arc side too (a line running into an arc, or two arcs). The point stays at the sharp corner, so the numbers you drew stay as they were; an arc side is cut back along its own circle to meet the round. Where the sides already meet smoothly there is no corner. Set it in the form’s points table (the Radius column), or give Radius on the status bar while drawing: every corner gets it, and a corner whose sides are too short gets the most that fits. An open polyline’s ends have no corner. On the command line: chipload shape add P polyline --points "0,0 100,0 100,60 0,60" --radii "0 5 5 0", shape set P ID --radii ….
  • Arc (A): where it starts, where it ends, then a point it passes through (or type its radius: it bows to the cursor’s side). It becomes an open polyline of one or two arc segments. Another way, from the arrow on its button:
    • Centre, start, end: the centre, where it starts (the radius; type a length for it), then where it ends: only the direction from the centre counts. The arc goes round the way you move the cursor from the start, so move the other way round for the other side. Type <90 for the end at 90° on the circle.
  • Slot (U): the centres of its two ends, then its width (a click, or type it). It is a rectangle whose corner radius is half its width; it shows as Slot, and stays one when you change its width. Another way, from the arrow on its button:
    • Arc slot: the centre of its arc, where its centre line starts (the radius; type a length for it), where it ends (the way you move round, as the arc by its centre does; <120 types the end’s angle), then its width (a click, or type it). It becomes a closed polyline of arcs with round ends, for clamp and adjustment slots; afterwards it is edited as a polyline. chipload shape add P arc-slot --center 0,0 --radius 40 --start-deg 30 --end-deg 150 --width 8.
  • Polygon (N): the centre, then a corner. Its number of sides is on the status bar while you draw (6 to start with, then the last you chose). Its form has the radius to the corners and the size across flats, either of which sets the other (a hexagon nut’s spanner size), and the angle of its first corner. chipload shape add P polygon --center 50,30 --sides 6 --across-flats 13. Two more ways, from the arrow on its button:
    • Centre and side: the centre, then the middle of a side: the size across flats is twice that distance (type it as a length: 6.5 for a 13 mm nut). Snapping perpendicular from the centre onto a line makes a polygon that touches it.
    • On an edge: the two ends of one side. The polygon lies to the side’s left, so click from left to right for a polygon above the side, right to left for one below.
  • Spline (B): click the points a smooth curve passes through, as for a polyline: Enter or a double-click finishes it open, clicking the first point closes it, Backspace drops the last point. The preview shows the curve through the cursor. It bends evenly through each point, never kinks or loops between two, and keeps its points: drag one, or change them in its form (a table of X, Y, as a polyline’s, and Closed). Snapping to a spline takes its points (as ends), the nearest point on it, and tangents and perpendiculars. It is cut as lines and arcs within 0.01 mm of the curve, since controllers have no spline move. On the command line: chipload shape add P spline --points "0,0 20,15 45,5 60,20" [--closed], and shape set P ID --points … --closed. Moving, turning, mirroring and scaling move its points, and the curve follows exactly.

Pick the tool in the ribbon’s DRAW group (the small buttons; hover over one for its name and key) or by its key, then click on the canvas. With no tool active, Enter starts the last one again.

A button with a small triangle in its corner draws its shape more than one way. Click the triangle, or right-click the button, for the list; the way you choose stays on the button until you close Chipload. The Draw menu lists them under the shape. Pressing the key again before the first click moves to the next way (R, R, R: two corners, centre, aligned; C, A, U and N go round theirs the same way) and the status bar names it. Clicks snap to the drawing (below), else to the grid, whose step the status bar gives (“grid 1 mm”).

Snapping

Within 8 pixels of the cursor, a click or a drag lands exactly on a point of the drawing, marked with a small diamond and named on the status bar (“intersection of #3 and #4”):

  • End of a line or arc, intersection where two cross or touch, centre of an arc or circle, quadrant (an arc’s points at 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°), middle of a line or arc.
  • While drawing a polyline or measuring, from the last point: perpendicular to a line or arc, and tangent to an arc.
  • On a line or arc: the nearest point of it, when none of the others is near.

The nearest of them wins; at the same spot, they count in the order above. Hold Alt to turn snapping off.

Exact values

While a tool waits for a point, type a number: an entry opens beside the cursor. Enter uses it, Esc closes it.

TypeFor
120,40the point X 120, Y 40
@30,-1030 to the right of the last point and 10 down
3030 from the last point, towards the cursor
30<4530 from the last point at 45° (counter-clockwise from the X axis)
<45at 45° from the last point, as far as the cursor is (an arc by its centre: its end at 45°)

Lengths are in the units the app shows, unless you name them: 1/2in, 12mm. Every value can be a sum: 600/4,18+0.2, (100-6)/2, <360/7 (arithmetic). Where the decimal point is a comma, separate X and Y with a semicolon: 12,5;30. For a circle, a plain length after the centre is its radius; for an aligned rectangle, after the edge, its width. The preview shows where the value lands before you press Enter; a value that can’t be used turns red and the status bar says why.

Beside the cursor, the size being drawn: the line’s length and angle, a circle’s radius, a rectangle’s length × width. Hold Shift to keep the line from the last point at a multiple of 15° (0° and 90° for straight lines), its length on the grid.

Editing operations

The ribbon’s MODIFY group (and the Modify menu) holds tools that make new shapes from drawn ones. Shapes from a DXF, a picture or a text can’t be changed by them (explode a DXF first, Explode).

  • Offset (O): select the shapes (or click one with the tool), then move the cursor to the side you want, outside or inside a closed shape, left or right of an open one: the preview follows at the cursor’s distance on the grid (Alt: off the grid). Click, or type the distance and press Enter. The status bar has the corners, round (the exact offset, arcs round the corners) or sharp (the sides extended until they meet, as a frame), and a Count for several rings, each the distance further. The shapes stay; the new ones go on the same layer and are selected. A circle, a rectangle or slot, and a polygon inward or with sharp corners keep their kind; anything else becomes a polyline with true arcs. On the command line: chipload shape offset P 1 2 --distance 3 [--side inside] [--corners sharp] [--count 2].
  • Fillet and Chamfer: click a corner of any drawn shape (it is marked when the cursor is near it; no need to select the shape first, and a selected shape’s corners come first) to round it with an arc of the Radius on the status bar, or cut it straight across, the Distance back along each side. Shift+click does every corner. Fillet rounds any corner, beside an arc too; Chamfer only cuts corners between two straight sides. A radius too large for the sides is refused with the most that fits (two corners on one side share it); a Radius of 0 makes a rounded corner sharp again. A polyline’s corner gets that Radius (above), so it stays editable; a rectangle rounded all round keeps its kind (its corner radius); any other shape becomes a polyline and keeps its number, so operations still cut it. Chamfer leaves rounded corners as they are. chipload shape fillet P 3 --radius 2 [--corner 0], chipload shape chamfer P 3 --distance 1.
  • Fillet between two shapes: two open drawn polylines (lines and arcs too) that meet, cross or fall short are joined by a round: each is trimmed or extended to it, on the part you pick, and they join into one polyline as after a Trim when they’re on one layer (the round goes on the first one’s layer). Between two lines the round stays the corner’s Radius. A radius of 0 trims or extends them to meet in a sharp corner; Chamfer between two lines cuts across, the Distance back from where they meet. On the command line, a point near each part to keep: chipload shape fillet P 3 --with 4 --radius 5 --keep 10,0 --keep 0,10 (or shape chamfer … --distance 2). In the window, with Fillet (or Chamfer): click the first shape on the part to keep (away from its corners), then move to the second: the round and both shapes trimmed or extended are shown before you click it, on its part to keep; if no round fits, the status bar says why. Escape, or a click on empty space, forgets the first pick. One undo step.
  • Trim (X), Extend and Break work on the drawn shape under the cursor; no need to select it first. Trim shows the piece it will remove before you click, Extend the shape grown to the next one ahead (click near the end to extend), Break a mark where it will split. Each click is one step, and the changed shape is selected; a click on empty space deselects it, another ends the tool, as Esc does. On the command line:
    • chipload shape trim P ID --at X,Y removes the piece of a drawn shape at that point, between the nearest places where shown shapes cross it: an open shape cut in its middle becomes two, a closed one opens, a circle becomes an arc. What is left joins the drawn open shapes on its layer whose ends meet its own into one shape (closed when it comes round): a slot trimmed in half by a line, and the line’s ends trimmed, is one closed shape. Construction geometry never joins, and nothing joins where three ends meet.
    • chipload shape extend P ID --end start|end lengthens a straight end along itself to the next shape ahead.
    • chipload shape break P ID --at X,Y splits an open shape there, or opens a closed one.
    • The shape keeps its id for the first piece, so operations follow it.
  • Union, Subtract and Intersect combine closed drawn shapes, with true arcs where they have arcs: a union is everything any of them covers, a subtraction the first shape with the others cut out of it, an intersection what they all cover. The result takes the first shape’s number, so operations that cut it follow; the other shapes go, and operations that cut those are flagged until you choose their geometry again. A result that is one of the shapes unchanged (a hole cut where a circle already lies, a shape a union swallows whole) stays as drawn, a circle still a circle; anything else becomes a polyline. A ring of shapes united gives the outline and the hole inside as two shapes. Open shapes are refused, and so is a result with nothing left. In the window: select the shapes, then Union or Intersect acts at once (the shape drawn first keeps its number). For Subtract, select the shapes to cut out, choose Subtract, then click the shape to cut them from: the preview shows the result under the cursor. On the command line: chipload shape union P 1 2 3, chipload shape subtract P 1 4 5 (4 and 5 cut out of 1), chipload shape intersect P 1 2.
  • Mirror (I): select drawn shapes, then click two points of the mirror line (they snap; Shift keeps the line to 15° steps), or click a straight line, such as a construction centre line, to mirror about it. The preview shows the result before the last click. With Keep the original (on the status bar, on to start with) the mirrored shapes are copies; a half outline whose two ends lie on the line joins its mirror image into one closed shape that keeps its number, so drawing half a part against a centre line and mirroring it gives the whole outline, ready to cut. Unticked, the shapes flip in place and keep their numbers, and operations’ start points and tabs on them follow. On the command line: chipload shape mirror P 3 4 --line "0,0 0,100" [--copy], or --about-shape 7 for a drawn line.
  • Scale by a length: when you know what a length on the drawing should be (two holes 100 mm apart, a side 450 mm long), select what to scale, choose Scale in MODIFY and click the two ends of that length. They snap, and the first one stays where it is. The entry beside the cursor shows the length now; type the length it should be (units and arithmetic work, as in every field) and press Enter, or click a third point: its distance from the first becomes the new length, so a length can be taken from another shape. The two points don’t have to lie on the selection. Esc goes back a step. It scales whatever the box’s corners scale: drawn shapes, a text, a picture, a group or a whole DXF, which is handy for a drawing or a traced picture that came in at the wrong size. On the command line: chipload shape scale P 3 4 --reference "10,10 110,12" --length 120, and for a DXF chipload place P --reference "0,0 254,0" --length 100.
  • Stretch: a different factor along X and along Y, to make shapes wider without making them taller. Straight sides stretch exactly. A rectangle square to the axes stays a rectangle; a spline goes through its stretched points. A stretched circle or arc is part of an ellipse, which can’t be cut as one arc, so a circle, a polygon, a turned rectangle or a polyline with arcs becomes a polyline of lines and arcs that follows the stretched curve within 0.01 mm; it keeps its number, so the operations that cut it follow. Corner radii stay the radii you typed. Equal factors scale evenly, and a circle stays a circle. Only drawn shapes stretch: explode a DXF (chipload dxf explode) or turn a text’s letters into shapes first. In the window: drag the middle of a side of the box round the selected shapes, or untick Keep proportions and type a width or height (both below). The status bar names the shapes that became polylines. On the command line: chipload shape stretch P 3 4 --x 1.25 (about the middle of their box), --width 500 --about 0,0, --y 0.8.
  • Arrays add copies of drawn shapes in a grid or round a centre; the originals stay and the copies go on their layers with new numbers. A grid counts the originals in its columns and rows and spaces them centre to centre (negative goes left or down). A polar array counts the originals too: over 360° the copies spread evenly round, over a smaller angle the first and last sit at its ends; each copy turns with its place unless you say not to. This adds shapes to the drawing; a part’s instances are different: they repeat the whole part with its operations. In the window: select the shapes, choose Array or Polar array, and set the copies under Selection; for a polar array click the centre on the canvas (it snaps as drawing does). The preview shows the copies; Create adds them and ends the tool. Cancel, Esc or the tool’s button again ends it without copies. On the command line: chipload shape array P 2 3 --columns 4 --rows 2 --dx 30 --dy 20, chipload shape array-polar P 5 --count 6 --center 100,80 [--angle-deg 180] [--no-rotate].
  • Align and Distribute sit under Selection when drawn shapes are selected. Align lines up each shape’s box (its left or right side, top or bottom, or middle) with that of the shape you picked first, which stays put, however many times you align the same selection. Shapes picked all at once with a box have no first: they line up with the box round them all. With To the stock they line up with the stock’s box (then one shape is enough). Hover over Align to see which applies. Distribute, with three shapes or more, evens out the gaps between their boxes across or up and down; the outermost two stay put. Shapes move whole and keep their numbers. Pieces that join into one outline (a slot drawn as four lines and arcs) line up as one, and so does a group with its texts. On the command line, the first shape named stays put: chipload shape align P 2 3 --to left|centre|right|top| middle|bottom [--stock], chipload shape distribute P 2 3 4 --horizontal (or --vertical).
  • New shapes on a layer that an operation selects by its name are cut by that operation too, as a shape drawn there is; the status bar names those operations.

Construction geometry

Lines and circles that help you draw (a centre line to mirror about, a circle to place holes on, a guide at 45°) and are never cut.

  • Draw one: with a drawing tool active, press K (or tick Construction on the status bar): the shapes you draw next are construction geometry, until you press K again.
  • Make a shape one, or an ordinary shape again: select drawn shapes and press K, tick Construction under Selection, or right-click.
  • It shows finely dotted and faint, and the cursor snaps to it like any shape: its ends, middles, centres and where it crosses other shapes.
  • It is never cut: it never joins other shapes into a contour, an operation never picks it up from a layer, and an operation that names it by its number is refused until it cuts something else. Trim and extend can use it as the edge to cut back to; offsets, copies, pasted copies and arrays of it are construction geometry too.
  • Exported only when you ask (chipload dxf export … --construction, or the checkbox), on a layer of its own named after its layer with _construction.
  • On the command line: chipload shape add P polyline --points "0,0 100,0" --open --construction, chipload shape set P 4 --construction false.

Editing

  • Moving with the mouse: drag a drawn shape to move it; if it is selected, the whole selection moves with it. The point you grab snaps as drawing does, so a corner can be dropped exactly on another shape’s corner or a crossing; Shift keeps the move to 15° steps, Alt turns snapping off. Select several with a box first (drag on empty canvas, the canvas). Shapes from a DXF, a picture or a text move as a whole with their own handles.
  • Handles: drag a selected shape’s handles to change it. A drag snaps as drawing does; hold Alt to turn snapping off. Hold Shift to keep a point (or a centre, or a corner) to 15° steps from where it was, so straight across, up or at 45°; on a turn handle or a polygon’s corner, Shift turns the shape in 15° steps. Shapes whose ends meet make one outline (an open polyline closed by another): a click selects the whole outline for operations, and the handles and form are the clicked shape’s.
  • Several points at once: on a selected polyline or spline, Ctrl+click (⌘ on a Mac) its points to pick them; they turn solid. Drag any of them and all move together, as one undo step (Shift keeps the move to 15° steps). Ctrl+click a picked point to leave it out; Escape, or a click elsewhere, lets go of them. Rounded corners keep their radius; a move that leaves no room for one is held back, and the form says why.
  • Polyline segments: on a selected polyline, drag one of its lines (anywhere, or its round middle handle) to move that segment; the segments either side stretch to stay joined. Ctrl+drag inserts a new point there instead, and Shift+drag bends the segment into an arc. The dashed box round the polyline moves it whole.
  • Exact values: under Selection in the Properties panel, a selected shape has its exact numbers and its layer.
  • Lengths on the canvas: a selected drawn shape shows its lengths beside it: each straight side of a polyline, a rectangle’s length and width, a circle’s or polygon’s radius (a side too short on screen to hold its label shows none until you zoom in). Click one, type the new length and press Enter: a polyline side keeps its start and its end moves along it, a rectangle keeps its centre.
  • Rounded corners: a rectangle’s Corner radius rounds all four corners with true arcs (G2/G3 in the program). It can be at most half the shorter side: half of it rounds the short ends fully, into a slot. A larger radius is refused rather than shrunk, and a corner handle won’t drag the rectangle smaller than its corners allow; lower the radius first. The reset button makes the corners sharp again.
    • Each corner its own: tick Each corner under Corners in a rectangle’s or a polygon’s form for a radius per corner, numbered counter-clockwise from the first (a rectangle’s from its lower left as drawn at 0°). The shape stays a rectangle or a polygon, its size, angle and sides still editable. Unticked, one radius rounds every corner (for a polygon, Corner radius). Fillet on one corner of either sets that corner’s radius. On the command line: --radii "0 10 10 0" (--corner-radius sets every corner alike).
  • Move, turn and size: select a polyline, or several drawn shapes (Ctrl+click), and a dashed box shows round them: drag the square in the middle to move them, the round handle beyond the right side to turn them (in steps of 15°; hold Alt for 0.1°), a corner to scale them evenly, the opposite corner staying put, or the middle of a side to stretch them one way, the opposite side staying put. One circle or rectangle keeps its own handles instead. The shapes keep their ids, so the operations that cut them follow, and so do the operations’ start points, corner choices and tabs placed by hand on them (also with shape move, turn and scale, and when you change one shape through its own handles or form: a corner’s choice stays with that corner of a rectangle you move or resize).
  • Position, Size and Turn: a selected polyline’s or spline’s form, and the Selection section when several drawn shapes are selected, have Position (the lower-left corner of the box round them: type a value to move them there), Size (the box’s width and height) and Turn: ⟲ 90, ⟳ 90, or type an angle and press Enter. With Keep proportions ticked (as it starts), a new width or height scales them evenly about the middle of their box; unticked, it stretches them one way. A polyline keeps no angle of its own; turning moves its points.
  • In the Browser: under Drawing, the Circles, Rectangles, Polylines, Polygons and Splines groups list their shapes in a folder per layer. Click a row to select the shape (Ctrl+click adds or removes); right-click to rename, copy or delete it. A group with more than 20 shapes starts closed.
  • Names: give a drawn shape a Name in its form (or Rename… in the Browser); the Browser shows it, with its number beside it. Empty: it’s shown by its number. Copies start without a name.
  • Copy and paste: Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V. Draw > Paste puts the copy 10 mm right and up; Ctrl+V over the canvas pastes at the cursor.
  • Mirror: Draw > Mirror left–right and Mirror up–down flip shapes about the middle of their box; Mirror in MODIFY mirrors about any line (above).
  • Delete: Del (or right-click > Delete). Operations that cut the shape are flagged. Del also deletes a text, a picture or a DXF selected as a whole (by a click on a letter, or on its Browser row), as its Remove does; a box round a sign takes its words with the frame. A shape picked out of an imported DXF or traced picture stays: it changes only with its file.
  • Undo: Ctrl+Z.

Imported shapes can’t be edited: they change only with the DXF, unless you explode it. Lines whose ends almost meet can be joined into one shape with Draw > Join ends… (same page).

The Compass

The Compass moves, turns and copies the selection from one handle: two arrows (its X and Y axes), a ring and a centre. Select drawn shapes, a group, or a whole picture, text, 3D model or drawing, then press Q (also MODIFY > Compass in the ribbon, or Compass in the canvas’s right-click menu). It shows on the middle of the selection, and the selection’s own handles hide while it shows. Q again or Esc hides it.

  • An arrow: drag to move along that axis. The distance goes in steps of the grid, or to where a point of another shape lies along the axis.
  • The ring: drag to turn about the centre, in steps of 15° (Alt: 0.1°).
  • The centre: drag to move freely; the centre snaps to points, so a point of the selection lands exactly on a point of another shape.
  • Exact values: double-click an arrow, the ring or the centre and type the distance, the angle, or ΔX and ΔY (two numbers), then Enter.
  • Put the Compass on a point first: tap Space (press and let go). The Compass turns grey: drag its centre onto a corner, a hole’s centre or a line’s middle (the selection’s own points count too), or drag an arrow to turn its axes, which lie along a line under the cursor. Tap Space again to lock it: the next drag is measured from there. Holding Space while dragging still moves the view.
  • Right-drag an arrow, the ring or the centre, and choose when you let go: Move here (or Turn here), Copy here (the copy is then selected, so you can place the next one), Linear array… (from an arrow or the centre: count and step, the step as dragged) or Circular array… (from the ring: count and angle, round the Compass’s centre, the angle as dragged). The arrays show their copies before Create. The menu starts with the dragged distance or angle (ΔX and ΔY from the centre), selected: type an exact value over it before choosing, or press Enter to move or turn by it.
  • Right-click the centre or an arrow (without dragging) for To point or To centre point, then click: the selection moves so that the Compass’s centre comes to the clicked point, snapping as when dragging (Alt: no snapping), or to the centre of the shape you click (a circle’s or an arc’s centre, else the middle of its box). From the centre it moves in X and Y; from an arrow only along that axis, as far as the point lies along it. The outline shows where it will go while you point; Esc cancels. Unlocked, the same moves the Compass itself the same way, and the selection stays.

Every move or turn is one undo step, and the shapes keep their ids, so the operations that cut them follow. Copies and arrays are for drawn shapes; a picture, text, 3D model or whole drawing only moves and turns. The command line does the same with shape move, shape turn --about, shape copy, shape array-linear and shape array-polar.

On the command line

chipload shape add part.chipload circle --center 40,30 --radius 5 --layer HOLES   # prints its id
chipload shape add part.chipload rectangle --center 80,30 --length 40 --width 10 --angle-deg 15
chipload shape set part.chipload 9 --corner-radius 3  # round the rectangle's corners
chipload shape add part.chipload polyline --points "0,0 50,0 50,20" --bulges "0 1" --open
chipload shape set part.chipload 8 --radius 6         # change a value or the layer
chipload shape set part.chipload 8 --name "Lid"       # its name in the Browser ("" takes it away)
chipload shape copy part.chipload 8 9 --dx 25 --dy 0  # copies, with new ids
chipload shape array-linear part.chipload 8 --count 5 --step 20,10   # a line of copies, any direction
chipload shape mirror part.chipload 8 9 --axis x      # flip in place; ids kept
chipload shape move part.chipload 8 9 --dx 10 --dy -5
chipload shape turn part.chipload 8 9 --angle-deg 30  # about the middle of their box (or --about X,Y)
chipload shape scale part.chipload 8 --factor 1.5
chipload shape scale part.chipload 8 --reference "0,0 30,0" --length 45   # A–B becomes 45 long, A stays
chipload shape stretch part.chipload 8 9 --x 1.5      # wider, not taller (also --y, --width, --height, --about)
chipload shape remove part.chipload 8
chipload shape join part.chipload --gap 0.05   # join ends that almost meet

Bulges are as in DXF: tan(sweep / 4), 0 straight, 1 a counter-clockwise half circle, −1 clockwise.
