Chipload 0.28.1 user guide

Drawing shapes

Chipload draws the parts it cuts, with or without a DXF:

  • Circle (key C): the centre, then a point on it.
  • Rectangle (R): two opposite corners. It keeps its centre, length and width, a rotation, and a corner radius (0: sharp corners).
  • Polyline (L): points joined by straight or arc segments, open or closed. Click the first point again to close it; Enter or a double-click finishes it open.
  • Arc (A): where it starts, where it ends, then a point it passes through (or type its radius: it bows to the cursor’s side). It becomes an open polyline of one or two arc segments.
  • Slot (S): the centres of its two ends, then its width (a click, or type it). It is a rectangle whose corner radius is half its width; it shows as Slot, and stays one when you change its width.
  • Polygon (N): the centre, then a corner. Its number of sides is on the status bar while you draw (6 to start with, then the last you chose). Its form has the radius to the corners and the size across flats, either of which sets the other (a hexagon nut’s spanner size), and the angle of its first corner. chipload shape add P polygon --center 50,30 --sides 6 --across-flats 13.

Pick the tool in the ribbon’s DRAW group (the small buttons; hover over one for its name and key) or by its key, then click on the canvas. With no tool active, Enter starts the last one again. Clicks snap to the drawing (below), else to the grid, whose step the status bar gives (“grid 1 mm”).

Snapping

Within 8 pixels of the cursor, a click or a drag lands exactly on a point of the drawing, marked with a small diamond and named on the status bar (“intersection of #3 and #4”):

  • End of a line or arc, intersection where two cross or touch, centre of an arc or circle, quadrant (an arc’s points at 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°), middle of a line or arc.
  • While drawing a polyline or measuring, from the last point: perpendicular to a line or arc, and tangent to an arc.
  • On a line or arc: the nearest point of it, when none of the others is near.

The nearest of them wins; at the same spot, they count in the order above. Hold Alt to turn snapping off.

Exact values

While a tool waits for a point, type a number: an entry opens beside the cursor. Enter uses it, Esc closes it.

TypeFor
120,40the point X 120, Y 40
@30,-1030 to the right of the last point and 10 down
3030 from the last point, towards the cursor
30<4530 from the last point at 45° (counter-clockwise from the X axis)

Lengths are in the units the app shows, unless you name them: 1/2in, 12mm. Where the decimal point is a comma, separate X and Y with a semicolon: 12,5;30. For a circle, a plain length after the centre is its radius. The preview shows where the value lands before you press Enter; a value that can’t be used turns red and the status bar says why.

Beside the cursor, the size being drawn: the line’s length and angle, a circle’s radius, a rectangle’s length × width. Hold Shift to keep the line from the last point at a multiple of 15° (0° and 90° for straight lines), its length on the grid.

Editing operations

The ribbon’s MODIFY group (and the Modify menu) holds tools that make new shapes from drawn ones. Shapes from a DXF, a picture or a text can’t be changed by them (explode a DXF first, Explode).

  • Offset (O): select the shapes (or click one with the tool), then move the cursor to the side you want, outside or inside a closed shape, left or right of an open one: the preview follows at the cursor’s distance on the grid (Alt: off the grid). Click, or type the distance and press Enter. The status bar has the corners, round (the exact offset, arcs round the corners) or sharp (the sides extended until they meet, as a frame), and a Count for several rings, each the distance further. The shapes stay; the new ones go on the same layer and are selected. A circle, a rectangle or slot, and a polygon inward or with sharp corners keep their kind; anything else becomes a polyline with true arcs. On the command line: chipload shape offset P 1 2 --distance 3 [--side inside] [--corners sharp] [--count 2].
  • Fillet and Chamfer: select one drawn shape (or click it with the tool), then click a corner (it is marked when the cursor is near it) to round it with an arc of the Radius on the status bar, or cut it straight across, the Distance back along each side. Shift+click does every corner. A corner is a point between two straight sides; a radius too large for the sides is refused with the most that fits (two corners on one side share it). A rectangle rounded all round keeps its kind (its corner radius); any other shape becomes a polyline and keeps its number, so operations still cut it. chipload shape fillet P 3 --radius 2 [--corner 0], chipload shape chamfer P 3 --distance 1.
  • Trim (X), Extend and Break work on the drawn shape under the cursor; no need to select it first. Trim shows the piece it will remove before you click, Extend the shape grown to the next one ahead (click near the end to extend), Break a mark where it will split. Each click is one step, and the changed shape is selected; a click on empty space deselects it, another ends the tool, as Esc does. On the command line:
    • chipload shape trim P ID --at X,Y removes the piece of a drawn shape at that point, between the nearest places where shown shapes cross it: an open shape cut in its middle becomes two, a closed one opens, a circle becomes an arc. What is left joins the drawn open shapes on its layer whose ends meet its own into one shape (closed when it comes round): a slot trimmed in half by a line, and the line’s ends trimmed, is one closed shape. Construction geometry never joins, and nothing joins where three ends meet.
    • chipload shape extend P ID --end start|end lengthens a straight end along itself to the next shape ahead.
    • chipload shape break P ID --at X,Y splits an open shape there, or opens a closed one.
    • The shape keeps its id for the first piece, so operations follow it.
  • Union, Subtract and Intersect combine closed drawn shapes, with true arcs where they have arcs: a union is everything any of them covers, a subtraction the first shape with the others cut out of it, an intersection what they all cover. The result takes the first shape’s number, so operations that cut it follow; the other shapes go, and operations that cut those are flagged until you choose their geometry again. A result that is one of the shapes unchanged (a hole cut where a circle already lies, a shape a union swallows whole) stays as drawn, a circle still a circle; anything else becomes a polyline. A ring of shapes united gives the outline and the hole inside as two shapes. Open shapes are refused, and so is a result with nothing left. In the window: select the shapes, then Union or Intersect acts at once (the shape drawn first keeps its number). For Subtract, select the shapes to cut out, choose Subtract, then click the shape to cut them from: the preview shows the result under the cursor. On the command line: chipload shape union P 1 2 3, chipload shape subtract P 1 4 5 (4 and 5 cut out of 1), chipload shape intersect P 1 2.
  • Arrays add copies of drawn shapes in a grid or round a centre; the originals stay and the copies go on their layers with new numbers. A grid counts the originals in its columns and rows and spaces them centre to centre (negative goes left or down). A polar array counts the originals too: over 360° the copies spread evenly round, over a smaller angle the first and last sit at its ends; each copy turns with its place unless you say not to. This adds shapes to the drawing; the project’s grid of copies is different, it repeats the whole part with its operations. In the window: select the shapes, choose Array or Polar array, and set the copies under Selection; for a polar array click the centre on the canvas (it snaps as drawing does). The preview shows the copies; Create adds them and ends the tool. Cancel, Esc or the tool’s button again ends it without copies. On the command line: chipload shape array P 2 3 --columns 4 --rows 2 --dx 30 --dy 20, chipload shape array-polar P 5 --count 6 --center 100,80 [--angle-deg 180] [--no-rotate].
  • Align and Distribute sit under Selection when drawn shapes are selected. Align lines up each shape’s box (its left or right side, top or bottom, or middle) with the box round them all, or, with To the stock, with the stock’s (then one shape is enough). Distribute, with three shapes or more, evens out the gaps between their boxes across or up and down; the outermost two stay put. Shapes move whole and keep their numbers. On the command line: chipload shape align P 2 3 --to left|centre|right|top| middle|bottom [--stock], chipload shape distribute P 2 3 4 --horizontal (or --vertical).
  • New shapes on a layer that an operation selects by its name are cut by that operation too, as a shape drawn there is; the status bar names those operations.

Construction geometry

Lines and circles that help you draw (a centre line to mirror about, a circle to place holes on, a guide at 45°) and are never cut.

  • Draw one: with a drawing tool active, press K (or tick Construction on the status bar): the shapes you draw next are construction geometry, until you press K again.
  • Make a shape one, or an ordinary shape again: select drawn shapes and press K, tick Construction under Selection, or right-click.
  • It shows finely dotted and faint, and the cursor snaps to it like any shape: its ends, middles, centres and where it crosses other shapes.
  • It is never cut: it never joins other shapes into a contour, an operation never picks it up from a layer, and an operation that names it by its number is refused until it cuts something else. Trim and extend can use it as the edge to cut back to; offsets, copies and arrays of it are construction geometry too.
  • Exported only when you ask (chipload dxf export … --construction, or the checkbox), on a layer of its own named after its layer with _construction.
  • On the command line: chipload shape add P polyline --points "0,0 100,0" --open --construction, chipload shape set P 4 --construction false.

Editing

  • Moving with the mouse: drag a drawn shape to move it; if it is selected, the whole selection moves with it. The point you grab snaps as drawing does, so a corner can be dropped exactly on another shape’s corner or a crossing; Shift keeps the move to 15° steps, Alt turns snapping off. Select several with a box first (drag on empty canvas, the canvas). Shapes from a DXF, a picture or a text move as a whole with their own handles.
  • Handles: drag a selected shape’s handles to change it. A drag snaps as drawing does; hold Alt to turn snapping off.
  • Polyline segments: on a selected polyline, drag one of its lines (anywhere, or its round middle handle) to move that segment; the segments either side stretch to stay joined. Ctrl+drag inserts a new point there instead, and Shift+drag bends the segment into an arc. The dashed box round the polyline moves it whole.
  • Exact values: under Selection in the Properties panel, a selected shape has its exact numbers and its layer.
  • Lengths on the canvas: a selected drawn shape shows its lengths beside it: each straight side of a polyline, a rectangle’s length and width, a circle’s or polygon’s radius (a side too short on screen to hold its label shows none until you zoom in). Click one, type the new length and press Enter: a polyline side keeps its start and its end moves along it, a rectangle keeps its centre.
  • Rounded corners: a rectangle’s Corner radius rounds all four corners with true arcs (G2/G3 in the program). It can be at most half the shorter side: half of it rounds the short ends fully, into a slot. A larger radius is refused rather than shrunk, and a corner handle won’t drag the rectangle smaller than its corners allow; lower the radius first. The reset button makes the corners sharp again.
  • Move, turn and size: select a polyline, or several drawn shapes (Ctrl+click), and a dashed box shows round them: drag the square in the middle to move them, the round handle beyond the right side to turn them (in steps of 15°; hold Alt for 0.1°), a corner to scale them evenly, the opposite corner staying put. One circle or rectangle keeps its own handles instead. The shapes keep their ids, so the operations that cut them follow, and so do the operations’ start points, corner choices and tabs placed by hand on them (also with shape move, turn and scale, and when you change one shape through its own handles or form: a corner’s choice stays with that corner of a rectangle you move or resize).
  • Position and Turn: a selected polyline’s form, and the Selection section when several drawn shapes are selected, have Position (the lower-left corner of the box round them: type a value to move them there) and Turn: ⟲ 90, ⟳ 90, or type an angle and press Enter. A polyline keeps no angle of its own; turning moves its points.
  • In the Browser: under Drawing, the Circles, Rectangles, Polylines and Polygons groups list their shapes, in a folder per layer when there are several. Click a row to select the shape (Ctrl+click adds or removes); right-click to rename, copy or delete it. A group with more than 20 shapes starts closed.
  • Names: give a drawn shape a Name in its form (or Rename… in the Browser); the Browser shows it, with its number beside it. Empty: it’s shown by its number. Copies start without a name.
  • Copy and paste: Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V. Draw > Paste puts the copy 10 mm right and up; Ctrl+V over the canvas pastes at the cursor.
  • Mirror: Draw > Mirror left–right and Mirror up–down flip shapes about the middle of their box.
  • Delete: Del. Operations that cut the shape are flagged.
  • Undo: Ctrl+Z.

Imported shapes can’t be edited: they change only with the DXF, unless you explode it. Lines whose ends almost meet can be joined into one shape with Draw > Join ends… (same page).

On the command line

chipload shape add part.chipload circle --center 40,30 --radius 5 --layer HOLES   # prints its id
chipload shape add part.chipload rectangle --center 80,30 --length 40 --width 10 --angle-deg 15
chipload shape set part.chipload 9 --corner-radius 3  # round the rectangle's corners
chipload shape add part.chipload polyline --points "0,0 50,0 50,20" --bulges "0 1" --open
chipload shape set part.chipload 8 --radius 6         # change a value or the layer
chipload shape set part.chipload 8 --name "Lid"       # its name in the Browser ("" takes it away)
chipload shape copy part.chipload 8 9 --dx 25 --dy 0  # copies, with new ids
chipload shape mirror part.chipload 8 9 --axis x      # flip in place; ids kept
chipload shape move part.chipload 8 9 --dx 10 --dy -5
chipload shape turn part.chipload 8 9 --angle-deg 30  # about the middle of their box (or --about X,Y)
chipload shape scale part.chipload 8 --factor 1.5
chipload shape remove part.chipload 8
chipload shape join part.chipload --gap 0.05   # join ends that almost meet

Bulges are as in DXF: tan(sweep / 4), 0 straight, 1 a counter-clockwise half circle, −1 clockwise.
