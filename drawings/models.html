Chipload 0.36.0 user guide

3D models

A 3D model in an STL file, or a picture read as a depth map, can be carved into the board from the top as a relief: a sign with raised, rounded letters, a rosette, a coin, a landscape. The model lies on the board like a picture, seen from above, and is carved in three axes; nothing under an overhang is reached. (Design: docs/specs/stl-relief.md.)

3DBenchy by Creative Tools (CC0), 150 mm wide and 10 mm deep, with a rim filled out by Fuller at 90 %, a roughing pass with a 6 mm end mill, a smoothing pass and a 2 mm ball nose finish.

A relief in one step

Relief… (the ribbon’s WIZARDS group, or Wizards > Relief…) makes a whole relief, one question at a time, each needing only the ones before it:

  1. Model: an STL file, or a model already in the project.
  2. Turn and cut: which side faces up, quarter turns, and how much to keep from the top (as in Turn and cut).
  3. Size and place: width and depth, centred on the board or at a point; the start depth, how far below the board’s surface the model’s top lies (0 by default; a millimetre or so on a board that isn’t planed, so its uneven top doesn’t reach the relief); the board’s thickness, which the start depth and the depth must stay within.
  4. Round it: a rim round the model’s outline (the default; the Fuller slider fills it out towards a rubber band round the model) or round a box, rounded corners if you like: a flat at the relief’s depth, then a ramp up to the surface; or the relief cut out through the board with tabs.
  5. Tools: the ball nose that finishes it; an end mill to rough it first, ticked when the relief is deeper than the ball nose is wide, and Smooth the steps after it (a smoothing pass, ticked); for a cut-out, its end mill, by default the roughing one, so one tool does both.
  6. Check and make: what will be made, in cutting order, with the tools and tool changes.

From step 3 on, a picture of the board beside the questions shows the relief where it will lie, and the rim or cut-out round it. Make adds it all as one undo step, in a part of its own: the model, the relief with its rim, roughing and smoothing passes, the cut-out. A board fitted to the drawing grows to hold the rim. Afterwards they are ordinary shapes and operations.

From the command line, the same answers in the same order:

chipload relief add sign.chipload rose.stl --keep 50% --width 120 --depth 6 \
    --around box --margin 10 --ramp 45 --tool 2 --rough-tool 1 --smooth

--around is rim (the default), box or cut-out; --hull 50% fills an outline rim out towards the convex hull; --smooth adds the smoothing pass. --model ID uses a model already in the project; --up, --rotate, --at X,Y, --start-depth, --corner, --allowance and --end-mill answer the rest. The tools are project tool numbers.

Adding a model

In the window: 3D model in the ribbon’s DRAW group, File > Add 3D model…, Add 3D model… on the Browser’s Drawing folder, or drop an STL file on the window. File > Import… and File > Open… take an STL too, as a new project. The model starts half the board wide at the drawing’s origin, as deep as its own proportion gives but no deeper than half the board (then Keep proportions is off), and its properties open.

From the command line:

chipload model add sign.chipload rose.stl --width 120 --depth 6 --at 20,20
  • The file: binary or text STL. It is kept inside the project, as pictures are, so the project stays one file; a model over 20 MB says that the project gets as large.
  • --width: its width on the board; the height follows. Without it, the model keeps its own size.
  • --depth: how deep the relief goes, from the model’s top at the stock top to its lowest point. Reliefs are usually flatter than the model: a 14 mm tall model carved 4 mm deep. Without it, the depth is in proportion to the width, and follows the width when it changes.
  • --at X,Y: where the lower-left corner of its footprint goes; --rotate turns it about its centre, any angle.
  • --up: which of the model’s axes points up: z (the default), y, x, or one of them reversed (-z …). Many models are drawn with Y up.
  • --units inch: STL files carry no unit. The size on import is read in mm, or in inches with this; since the model is placed by its width, it matters only for that first size.
  • --keep: how much of the model to keep, from its top: 0.5 or 50% keeps the top half and cuts away the rest, its back (see below). By default all of it.
  • --layer: the layer for its outline; by default the file’s name.

The report says how many triangles it has, any without an area that were left out, and how many open edges its surface has: a gap in a model lets the tool fall through to the relief’s floor.

A relief from a picture

A grey picture where brightness is height, a depth map, is a 3D model too: the usual way CNC reliefs are drawn and shared. Add 3D model…, the Relief wizard and model add take a PNG, JPEG or BMP as well as an STL. White is the top and black the bottom; the picture is sized by its width, and its depth is yours (4 mm to start with).

chipload model add sign.chipload portrait.png --width 120 --depth 4 --background 10%
chipload model set sign.chipload 1 --smoothing 0.5mm --gamma 1.5
chipload relief add sign.chipload portrait.png --width 120 --depth 4 --tool 2 --rough-tool 1 --smooth

Its properties, and step 2 of the wizard, have four settings in place of Up:

  • White is high: untick for a depth map that has it the other way round.
  • Smoothing: a blur over the heights, its radius on the board (0.2 mm to start with): it takes pixel noise and a photo’s grain away; 0 keeps the picture as it is.
  • Background: the darkest greys up to this share are not the model. The outline goes round the rest, and the rim round that; at 0 % the whole picture is the model.
  • Gamma: above 1 flatter low ground and steeper tops, below 1 the other way.

A large picture is averaged onto at most 800 points along its longer side (0.15 mm apart on a 120 mm relief), finer than a small ball nose can cut. Everything else is as for an STL: place, turn, keep from the top, the Relief operation with its rim, roughing and smoothing.

Inflated shapes

A black-and-white picture (a logo, lettering, a silhouette) can be inflated instead: each black shape rises from its edge towards its middle, as if blown up, and the white round it is not the model. Set Kind to Inflated in the picture’s settings, or:

chipload model add sign.chipload logo.png --inflate --width 120 --depth 4
chipload model set sign.chipload 1 --profile straight --reach 3mm
chipload relief add sign.chipload name.png --inflate --reach 2mm --width 200 --depth 5 --tool 2
  • Threshold: greys darker than this are the shapes; Automatic picks it from the picture. Invert: white is the shape.
  • Profile: Round rises steeply at the edge and is flat on top, a cushion; Straight rises evenly, to a ridge along each stroke, as if chiselled.
  • Reach: how far in from the edge a shape keeps rising. Automatic takes the widest shape’s half-width, so nothing levels off and narrow shapes stay lower than wide ones. Set it, and everything wider levels off at the full depth: for lettering, about half a stroke’s width gives letters all of one height.
  • Smoothing takes the pixels’ steps off the edges.

A letter’s hole is an edge too, so an “O” rises from both sides of its ring.

From an iPhone: Portrait photos carry a depth map, but inside the HEIC photo, which Chipload doesn’t read (it says so). Save the depth map as a PNG first, with an app or a Shortcut that exports it; it is small (about 768 × 576) and soft at the edges, so a little smoothing and a background level help. For an object you can walk round, a 3D-scanning app’s STL gives far more.

Turn and cut

When a model is added, Turn and cut opens (and later Turn and cut… in its properties): the model in 3D on the left, the relief it gives seen from above on the right, both redrawn as you change them.

  • Drag the model to look at it from any side.
  • Face up: which of the model’s sides faces up: Z, Y, X or one of them reversed.
  • Turn on the board: a quarter turn either way.
  • Keep from top: the cut level (below). The part kept is coloured, the part cut away grey, and the plate is the level.
  • Width and Depth, as in the properties; the size it gives is under the relief.

OK changes the model as one undo step and draws its outline again; Cancel leaves it as it was. The views are drawn from a light copy of the model (at most 50 000 triangles), so a large file turns smoothly; the outline, the toolpath and the G-code always come from the full model.

Keeping the front: the cut level

A model made to be seen from every side (a figure, a head, an animal) would be carved from its top all the way down to its lowest point: most of the depth goes into its far side, which the tool can’t reach anyway, and the side you see comes out flat. Keep from top in its properties (model set sign.chipload 1 --keep 50%) cuts it level at that share of its height and keeps the part above: the front half of a head keeps its whole outline, and its depth and width are now that half’s. So set the cut level first, then the depth the board allows; a depth you typed stays when the cut level changes. Where the cut goes through the model, the relief is cut to its floor.

Its outline

The model’s footprint seen from above becomes a closed shape on its layer, drawn again whenever the model is moved or changed. Select it for a profile to cut the relief out of the board, or for a pocket that clears round it: click the outline, Ctrl+click a shape round it, then Pocket, and the outline stays an island in the pocket. The outline keeps its shape ids when it is drawn again, so the pocket still finds it after the model moves.

Changing and removing

In the window the model is a row of the Browser’s Drawing folder; a click selects its outline and shows its properties: the file, its size on the board, Position (the lower-left corner before the turn), Rotation, Width, Depth with Keep proportions, Up, Keep from top and Layer. Each change draws the outline again, as one undo step. Selected on the canvas it has a picture’s handles: drag the middle to move it, the round handle to turn it, a corner to size it. In the 3D view it shows faintly as its surface, until a simulation is shown. Remove (in its properties or on its row) takes the model and its outline; a relief of it stays, with a problem, until it carves another model.

chipload model set sign.chipload 1 --rotate 15 --depth 4
chipload model set sign.chipload 1 --proportional     # the depth follows the width again
chipload model list sign.chipload                     # file, up axis, place and size
chipload model remove sign.chipload 1                 # the model and its outline

chipload set sign.chipload drawing.models.0.width=100 works as well, and draws the outline again.

Carving it: the Relief operation

In the window: New relief in the model’s properties or on its row, Relief in the ribbon’s OPERATION group (the small button under Surface), or Operation > New Relief; the last two carve the selected model, or the first. A relief is made in the active part, with a ball nose when the project has one.

From the command line:

chipload tool add "Ball 3" --type ballnose --diameter 3     # then `tool use` it in the project
chipload op add sign.chipload relief --model 1             # a relief of model 1, within its outline
chipload set sign.chipload operations.0.stepover=0.1 operations.0.angle=0.785

A Relief carves the model in parallel lines across its area, each point as low as the tool can go without cutting into the model: the tool is lowered onto the model’s triangles until it first touches one. A ball nose gives the smoothest finish; a tapered ball nose reaches finer detail; an end mill or a V-bit works too, with their own marks.

  • Area: with --model, the model’s outline, with its rim round it. Other closed shapes can be selected for the area instead: a drawn rectangle, say. Without a rim the tool’s centre stays inside the area, which leaves a groove the tool’s width round the model and the board beyond untouched.
  • stepover: the distance between lines as a share of the tool; smaller is smoother and slower. With a ball nose a new relief takes the widest stepover that leaves ridges of 0.02 mm between its lines on flat ground, which wood doesn’t show: 20 % for a 2 mm ball, 16 % for 1/8“, 12 % for 6 mm. The relief’s form shows the ridges for the stepover you set. Finer only takes longer: a 2 mm ball at 10 % leaves ridges of 0.005 mm and takes about three times as long. Other tools start at 10 %.
  • angle: the lines’ direction in radians from X (0 by default). Lines at an angle to the model’s long edges leave fewer marks.
  • zigzag: back and forth (true, the default), or every line the same way, lifting between them.
  • start_depth: how far below the stock top the model’s top lies: a little on an unplaned board, so its uneven top doesn’t reach the relief. Its lowest point is the start depth plus the model’s depth, which the checker holds within the stock. The roughing pass still starts at the stock top, and a rim’s ramp still climbs to it.
  • Depth: the model’s depth. Changed in the relief’s properties, it is the model that changes, and every relief of it; a depth typed there turns the model’s Keep proportions off.

In the project file a relief reads:

{ "type": "relief", "name": "Relief 1", "enabled": true, "part": 1, "material": null,
  "model": 1, "select": { "model": 1, "shapes": null, "layer": null }, "tool": 2,
  "start_depth": 0.0, "end_depth": 5.0, "stepover": 0.1, "angle": 0.0, "zigzag": true,
  "feed": 1500.0, "plunge_feed": 500.0 }

model names the model it carves; end_depth is made again from the model’s depth whenever the project is read or changed.

A rim and a ramp

Round the model a relief cuts a rim: a flat at its full depth, then a ramp up to the board’s surface, so the model sits in a shallow dish instead of a trench:

 surface ─────╮                                   ╭───── surface
               ╲ ramp                       ramp ╱
                ╲        ╭── model ──╮          ╱
                 ╲______╱             ╲________╱
                   flat                  flat

Every line starts at the surface and runs down the ramp, so the tool never plunges to full depth at the area’s edge, and its shank stays clear of the outer wall. The roughing pass clears the rim with the rest.

  • Round: the model’s outline (the flat follows it, round at the corners) or a box, the rectangle round the model, with rounded corners if you like.
  • Flat width: how far the flat reaches from the outline or the box. 0 starts the ramp right at it.
  • Fuller (outline only): how far the outline is filled out before the flat goes round it. At 0 % the rim follows every notch of the model’s outline; higher fills narrow notches first, then wider ones, bridging them with round edges, and joins pieces that lie close together; at 100 % it goes round the model like a rubber band, its convex hull. The ramp keeps its angle all the way round.
  • Ramp: its slope from horizontal, 10° to 90°. At 45° it reaches out as far as the relief is deep; 90° is a straight wall.

A new relief has a rim 5 mm round its outline with a 45° ramp. Relief… and relief add make the flat wider when there is a roughing pass: its end mill’s width and the allowance on both sides, rounded up to a whole mm, so the end mill reaches the floor all round the model. When a flat is narrower than that, the plan notes it. In the window it is the relief’s Rim section; untick it for none. While the relief or one of its passes is open, the canvas shows the rim as dashed lines in the relief’s colour: the outline as Fuller fills it out (bolder), the flat’s outer edge and where the ramp reaches the top. They follow the settings as you change them, without generating the toolpath. From the command line:

chipload set sign.chipload 'operations.0.rim={"shape":"box","width":8,"ramp":0.785,"corner_radius":4}'
chipload set sign.chipload operations.0.rim.width=3
chipload set sign.chipload operations.0.rim.hull=0.5   # fuller: halfway to the hull
chipload set sign.chipload operations.0.rim=null      # no rim

ramp is in radians (0.785 is 45°). Holes in the outline are cut to the depth. A board fitted to the drawing grows to hold the rim; on a board of fixed size, the plan notes when the ramp reaches past its edge, where it is cut off.

Along each line the tool stays within 0.01 mm of where the model needs it: more points are added where the surface bends. When the program is made, the path is checked against the model once more by a simpler test of its own; if anything would cut into the model, nothing is made and the report says where.

Roughing first

A finishing ball nose is small, and a relief can be deeper than it should cut in one go (the plan and the relief’s form warn when the relief is deeper than the tool is wide). A rim’s ramp spares the tool the plunges, but on the way down each line still shaves a strip as tall as the depth there. A roughing pass takes most of the material first with a larger tool. In the window: Add roughing pass ▸ on the relief’s row in the Browser (right-click), with your project’s and library’s end mills and plain ball noses; the pass shows under the relief, with its own properties. Remove on its row, or Delete with it selected, takes it away. From the command line:

chipload op add-roughing sign.chipload --op 1 --tool 1 --allowance 0.5mm
chipload set sign.chipload operations.0.roughing.step_down=2.5
chipload op remove-roughing sign.chipload --op 1
  • The tool: an end mill or a plain ball nose (not a V-bit or a tapered ball nose). Its feeds, step down and stepover come from the tool library’s cutting data, or else from the relief, half the tool’s diameter and 0.4.
  • Levels: it goes down one step_down at a time from the stock top to the relief’s lowest point, along the relief’s lines (stepover apart, at the relief’s angle), and cuts only where material is left above each level.
  • allowance: what it leaves on the model for the finish, 0.5 mm by default, all round, not only above: on steep sides too.
  • Never wider than the finish: its area is the relief’s, shrunk so its edge stays where the finishing tool reaches. The finish takes the narrow ring left inside the outline itself.

It is cut just before the relief, as pass Operation 1.1 (Relief 1, roughing), with its own tool; the program is checked to keep the allowance off the model. In the project file it is the relief’s roughing:

"roughing": { "tool": 1, "feed": 1500.0, "plunge_feed": 500.0, "step_down": 3.0,
  "stepover": 0.4, "allowance": 0.5, "enabled": true }

Smoothing the steps

Along its lines the roughing end mill follows the surface, but between them, on ground that rises across the lines, its flat bottom leaves a stair: steps one stepover wide and as tall as the slope makes them, 2.4 mm on a 45° ramp at 40 % of a 6 mm end mill. Smaller step-downs don’t change them. A small ball nose would take each step in one bite.

A smoothing pass takes them off: after the roughing, the roughing end mill follows the surface once more, no levels, with its lines across the roughing’s, and leaves the same allowance. Ground that rises along either set of lines comes out clean, so a box rim’s four ramps do; on curved ground the steps shrink to the smoothing’s stepover times the slope. A larger ball nose works too: it leaves small, even ridges everywhere, for one more tool change.

In the window: Add smoothing pass on the relief’s or the roughing pass’s row in the Browser (right-click), or in the roughing pass’s properties; on a relief without a roughing pass it is greyed out, its hover saying to add one first. The pass shows under the relief after the roughing, with its own properties (tool, stepover, the lines’ angle, allowance, feeds); Delete with it selected removes it. From the command line:

chipload op add-smoothing sign.chipload --op 1                 # the roughing tool
chipload op add-smoothing sign.chipload --op 1 --tool 3 --allowance 0.3mm
chipload op remove-smoothing sign.chipload --op 1
  • Defaults: the roughing pass’s tool, feeds and allowance; lines a quarter turn from the relief’s; stepover 15 % with an end mill, or with a ball nose the widest that leaves 0.1 mm ridges on flat ground.
  • Only after roughing: it follows the surface in one go, so it needs the roughing pass, ticked, before it; removing the roughing pass removes it too.

It is cut between the roughing and the relief, as Operation 1.2 (Relief 1, smoothing); the program is checked to keep the allowance off the model. In the project file it is the relief’s smoothing:

"smoothing": { "tool": 1, "feed": 1500.0, "plunge_feed": 500.0, "stepover": 0.15,
  "angle": 1.5708, "allowance": 0.5, "enabled": true }
