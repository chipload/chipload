Chipload 0.36.0 user guide

Signs and frames

An engraved sign is a few words with a frame round them, cut out of a board. New sign… (below) makes the whole sign, its operations too. Draw > Frame… only draws a frame: round the text or shapes you have selected, centred on them, with border lines inside it, or to a size as a blank, for a frame you cut with operations of your own. A sign you will make again can be kept in the shape library. (Design: docs/specs/shape-library.md.)

A sign in one step

New sign… (Sign in the ribbon’s WIZARDS group, or Wizards > New sign…) makes a whole sign: the words, a frame round them, and every operation that cuts it, in a part of its own named after the words (a new project’s empty part becomes it). The dialog draws the sign as it will be and lists the operations it adds. (Design: docs/specs/sign-maker.md.)

  • Words, Font, Height: the text, centred on the board. The font list is the text tool’s: your recently used fonts first (the last one picked is chosen), then the built-in fonts and your computer’s (Text). With shapes selected when you open it (a traced picture, a text, drawn shapes or a DXF’s), the sign goes round the selection instead: those shapes are carved with the V-bit (an operation named Carving) and the frame follows them, a picture traced again too. Write words instead goes back to new words. A raised sign round a picture’s or a DXF’s shapes can’t be made yet (its background pocket needs them grouped with the frame, and a group takes only drawn shapes and texts).
  • Frame: as for Frame… below: the kind, margin, corner, borders and their groove width. The frame follows the words afterwards.
  • Style:
    • Engraved: the letters V-carved;
    • Raised: the background inside the innermost border cut down round the letters with the end mill (Background depth, 3 mm by default), the letters and the border left standing; it needs a border. Inside corners round the letters keep the end mill’s radius;
    • Inlay: the letters as a V-carve inlay, the plug in a board of its own.
  • Edge: a Chamfer of that width round the outline, cut with the V-bit centred on the outline before the sign is cut out (the other half of the groove is in the waste).
  • V-bit, End mill: the project’s tools, then under From your library: your tool library’s (marked +). A library tool is copied into the project when you click Make sign, not before. A project without such a tool starts on the library’s. The V-bit carves the letters and the grooves, the end mill cuts the sign out (and clears a raised background).
  • Board: Fit round the sign (on in an empty project) fits the stock round the drawing with the margin, at the thickness.

It adds, in cutting order: Letters (or Background, or the inlay’s pocket), Borders (a follow path on the border lines as deep as the groove width needs), Edge chamfer, and Cut out (a profile outside the outline, through, with tabs). Feeds come from the tools’ cutting data. The words and the frame are one group: a click selects the sign, a drag moves it. All of it is one undo step, and every part of it is ordinary shapes and operations afterwards.

A frame round a text

  1. Write the text (Text) and leave it selected, or select any shapes, a traced picture for instance: the frame goes round all of them.
  2. Right-click the selection and choose Frame round the selection…, or Draw > Frame… (the way to a frame to a size, with nothing selected). Its values open under Selection, and the frame shows on the canvas as you change them.
  3. Pick a frame from the row of pictures, set the values, and click Add. The frame is added as one undo step, and its shapes are selected.

Clicking a shape while the tool is open makes it what the frame goes round (Ctrl+click adds one); a click on empty space, or on the previewed frame, changes nothing. Esc or Cancel ends the tool. The tool keeps your values until you close Chipload.

The values:

  • Margin: from the box round what is selected to the innermost line, so the words sit evenly inside the border nearest them (10 mm by default). The frame is centred on that box and made just large enough: an oval grows in proportion, an arched top grows taller, a plaque grows along its long side. Its size shows under the values.
  • Corner: the corner size (above). Unticked, it is a tenth of the frame’s shorter side, shown greyed; tick it to set your own. One too large for the frame is refused, with the largest that fits.
  • Borders: 0 to 3 lines inside the outline, each Gap further in (5 mm by default). They follow the outline’s shape: a notched frame’s borders are notched too.
  • Border corners (a rectangle with borders): the borders’ own corners, at the same gap, such as a round sign with a notched line inside. As the outline follows its shape.
  • Groove width: how wide a border’s groove will be at the surface: the V-bit’s width at the depth it cuts. The margin is then measured to the inner edge of the innermost groove, so the words keep their distance from the cut, not from the line. 0 (the default) measures to the line.
  • Double lines: each border two grooves, twice the groove width apart, which leaves a ridge as wide as a groove between them. It needs the groove width. The gap is then from one border’s inner line to the next border.
  • Layer, Border layer: the outline goes on frame, the borders on border, so that each can be cut by its own operation.

A frame is ordinary drawn shapes: a rounded or plain rectangle, a circle for a round oval, otherwise a polyline of lines and arcs. Operations cut it like any other shape.

The frame follows its text

A frame remembers what it goes round. Change the words, the font or the height of the text, or move it, and the frame is drawn again round it, with its borders, in the same undo step. Its shapes keep their ids, so the operations that cut them go on cutting them. A frame round a picture follows it when it is traced again, at another size or place; one round other shapes follows them when they move. A frame round only some of a text’s letters or a picture’s shapes keeps those shapes by id, and lets go when they are made again.

Select the frame (its outline or a border) and its values show under Selection, in a Frame card: the kind, margin, corner, borders and gap. Change them there and the frame is drawn again. A frame drawn to a size shows its width and height instead, and follows nothing.

The frame lets go, and becomes plain shapes that follow nothing, when:

  • you edit one of its shapes by hand: move it alone, drag a corner, change its values in its own form (moving the frame with its text keeps it following);
  • one of its shapes is deleted, or everything it went round is;
  • Detach in its card, on purpose.

A note on the status bar says so each time. Undo brings the frame back as it was.

An older Chipload refuses to open a project with a frame that follows something, and says why; detach the frame first to open the project there.

A frame to a size

With nothing selected, Draw > Frame… asks for the outline’s Width and Height instead of the margin, and the frame is centred on the stock: a blank for a sign, or a plaque to write on afterwards.

Cutting the sign

A sign is usually three operations, in this order:

  1. a V-carve on the text’s layer, with a V-bit;
  2. a Follow path on the border layer with the same V-bit: a groove along each border line, as wide as the V-bit is at that depth. Set its Groove width to the frame’s, and its end depth follows. (A V-carve of a border would carve the whole area inside it.);
  3. a Profile outside the frame layer with an end mill, to cut the sign out, with tabs.

The stock can sit round the drawing, sticking out equally on every side (Stock and work zero), so the board needs to be only as large as the sign.

From the command line

chipload text add sign.chipload "SIGN" --height 30 --at 150,80 --anchor center
chipload frame add sign.chipload --kind rounded --around-text 1 --borders 1 --corner 8 --margin 12
chipload frame add blank.chipload --kind oval --width 300 --height 150 --borders 2
chipload frame add sign.chipload --corners notch --around-text 1 --borders 1 --double --border-width 2
chipload frame add plate.chipload --kind banner --width 300 --height 90 --corner 25
chipload sign add sign.chipload "THE NÄSLUNDS" --font FreeSerif --frame rectangle --corners notch \
    --borders 1 --double --style engraved --edge chamfer:3 --vbit 2 --end-mill 1 --thickness 18
chipload frame kinds
chipload frame list sign.chipload                              # the frames that follow, with their values
chipload frame set sign.chipload 7 --margin 15 --borders 2     # change them: drawn again
chipload frame detach sign.chipload 7                          # plain shapes from now on

sign add takes the words, or instead --around, --around-text or --around-image to make the sign round what is drawn; it takes the frame’s options as frame add does (--frame for the kind), and --style engraved|raised|inlay, --relief, --edge none|chamfer:WIDTH, --vbit, --end-mill (project tool ids), --thickness and --board-margin (or --keep-stock); --json gives the new part, text, outline, borders, group and operations. --kind is one of the frames above (rectangle, oval, arched, double-arch, gable, shield, plaque, banner, octagon); --corners (sharp, round, notch, chamfer, step) picks a rectangle’s corners, and the older names rounded, notched, chamfered and stepped still work as kinds. --border-corners, --double and --border-width set the borders; frame set takes the same, and --border-corners-off to make the borders follow the outline again. --around takes shape ids, --around-text text ids (chipload text list), --around-image picture ids (chipload image list); without either, --width and --height give the outline’s size and --at its centre (the stock’s centre by default). The new shapes’ ids are printed, the outline’s first; --json gives them with the frame’s size and corner. frame set and frame detach take the id of the frame’s outline or of one of its borders. Every command that changes a text or a shape draws the frames that follow it again before it saves, and prints a line for any frame it let go of.
